## STR-008 physical burst transport correction — 2026-07-28

Result: implemented and technically verified; product-owner observation and acceptance remain pending.

The physical failure was host transport backlog, not Haskell gesture policy. The renderer already retained one latest DOM move per animation frame, but every frame called `onPointerEvent` while prior Wasm dispatches were unresolved. Because `main.js` did not return its enqueue promise, a physical mouse could queue many stale moves ahead of preview and pointerup even though RAF sampling itself was bounded.

The active Product/2 host now returns the enqueue promise and applies per-pointer mechanical backpressure: at most one move dispatch is unresolved and only the latest pending raw move is retained. Pointerup synchronously captures its final raw fact, drains the already-dispatched move and final move in order, and only then forwards up; native capture is not proactively released and remains governed by Haskell's `release_pointer` action. Cancel/lost-capture discard unsent moves and send one terminal fact after any dispatched move. Deactivation uses the same ordered terminal path while safely releasing capture from a detached surface. Rejected promises are settled, duplicate terminal facts and post-terminal moves are ignored, and no threshold, snap, fallback, commit, revision, or source rule moved into JavaScript.
